## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a bounty competition platform where you team up with an AI coding agent to build software for specific challenges. Each bounty describes what needs to be built, sets the rules, and offers a cash prize. When your entry ranks among the winners, you are paid in USDC.

Bounties follow a consistent flow:

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Open** | The bounty is live and you can start building. |
| **Submitted** | You send in your entry (a public repo/commit plus agent transcripts, as required). |
| **Checked** | Automated checks verify your submission meets the rules. |
| **Scored** | A sandbox grader runs every entry the same way, twice, for fair scoring. |
| **Paid** | Top-ranked entrants claim their USDC reward. |

Winners are decided by skill and how well entries meet the published criteria — not by chance. make.fun is operated by EZO Labs Inc., a Delaware corporation. (It is not related to makefun.ai or the `makefun` Python library.)

## How to Join a Bounty

Each bounty has its own page and competition kit. A typical flow looks like this:

1. **Pick a bounty.** Browse [make.fun](https://make.fun) and open a bounty that interests you. Check the reward, deadline, and required deliverable before you commit.
2. **Read the competition kit and rules.** The kit explains exactly what to build, how entries are scored, and what you must submit. Follow the instructions in `RULES.txt` (and any templates) closely.
3. **Build the deliverable with your AI agent.** Use a coding agent such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or GitHub Copilot to create the work the bounty asks for. If a starter is provided, build on it instead of reinventing the basics.
4. **Check and submit before the deadline.** Run the kit's checks (many bounties support local grading) to catch issues early, bundle what the bounty requires, and submit on time. If your entry is rejected or needs fixes, you can usually resubmit within the bounty's limits — read the status feedback carefully.

## Prizes and Escrow

When a bounty opens, its full prize is locked in escrow on Solana. That means the reward is secured up front before anyone competes.

- **Currency:** Rewards are paid in USDC (a dollar-pegged stablecoin) on Solana.
- **Claiming:** When winners are finalized, eligible entrants claim their USDC reward to their own Solana wallet.
- **Transparency:** The escrow program is open source at [github.com/makedotfun/prize-escrow](https://github.com/makedotfun/prize-escrow).
- **Entry fees:** Most bounties are free to enter. If a bounty has an entry fee, its page will say so, and that fee goes into the bounty's reward pool.

For the latest details on how payouts and escrow work for a specific bounty, always check that bounty's page and kit.

## Tips for Beginners

- **Read the rules first.** Build to the bounty's scoring criteria and required deliverables.
- **Check the deadline early.** Plan enough time for building, testing, and any resubmissions.
- **Use the kit's tools.** If the kit offers local checks or grading, run them before you submit.
- **Start simple, then iterate.** A clean, correct entry that meets the brief beats an overcomplicated one that misses requirements.
- **Submit early when you can.** Early submissions give you room to fix issues based on feedback or check results.
